# Lawyer Review Workflow

## 1. Purpose
The Lawyer Review Workflow module (`src/review`) provides a structured mechanism for a human reviewer (typically a legal professional) to inspect the model's findings (predictions, evidence, retrieval results) and record their own assessment. 

It acts as the final decision capture layer, ensuring that machine-generated outputs are clearly delineated from human judgements.

## 2. Reviewer Decisions
The workflow supports five explicit reviewer decisions:
- `ACCEPT`: The reviewer accepts the model finding as-is.
- `REJECT`: The reviewer rejects the model finding.
- `MODIFY`: The reviewer accepts the finding but with modifications (captured in rationales/notes).
- `REQUEST_MORE_EVIDENCE`: The reviewer requires additional context or evidence before deciding.
- `MARK_UNCERTAIN`: The reviewer flags the finding as ambiguous or uncertain.

## 3. Strict Separation of Model vs Human
> [!CAUTION]
> The workflow strictly separates **model-generated information** from **reviewer-generated information**.

A `ReviewRecord` encapsulates the immutable `PredictionContext` (the original model outputs). The reviewer's decision **never** overwrites the model's original prediction, score, evidence, or abstention states. This guarantees an untampered audit trail of what the system originally produced.

## 4. Provenance and Validation
Every `ReviewRecord` maintains an unbroken link to its origin:
- `prediction_id`
- `source_document_id`
- `clause_id`

The system validates that document/partition identifiers match between the request and the underlying context. Furthermore, any explicit evidence or retrieval references made by the reviewer are strictly validated against the known references generated by the model. Unknown or fabricated references are rejected.

## 5. Multiple Review Handling
The workflow supports multiple iterative reviews for the same prediction. Subsequent reviews do not silently overwrite earlier reviews; each is appended sequentially, generating a new `review_id` and `timestamp`.

## 6. Serialization
All data objects are serialized and deserialized into a deterministic, JSON-safe format to support downstream audit and storage systems. 

## 7. Legal-Safety Limitations
The review framework merely captures assessments of software-generated findings.
- **Do not generate legal conclusions.**
- **Do not label a clause illegal merely because a legal source was retrieved.**
- **Do not describe semantic similarity as precedent, legal authority, or legal equivalence.**
- **Do not convert retrieval scores into legal confidence.**
- **Do not convert model probability into probability of legal correctness.**
- **Do not claim the lawyer's decision is automatically legally correct.**

Outputs are strictly framed as: "Model finding", "Evidence presented", "Potentially relevant legal source", and "Reviewer assessment".

## 8. Test Coverage
The system includes 20 comprehensive unit tests verifying data separation, deterministic provenance, strict reference validation, invalid input rejection, and serialization precision.

## 9. Empirical Status
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **NO EMPIRICAL LAWYER EVALUATION PERFORMED.**

This workflow provides software infrastructure for human review. It has not been subjected to empirical legal testing, benchmark calibration, or lawyer agreement studies. No experimental validation of reviewer accuracy exists.
